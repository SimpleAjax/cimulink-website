The operating gap

Good people should not have to hunt for basic answers.

Most manufacturing complexity does not begin as a software problem. It begins when critical information is scattered across spreadsheets, calls, chat threads and individual memory. By the time a mismatch is visible, the customer commitment, material or margin may already be exposed.

The IMPACT framework

Make operational control a designed outcome.

A manufacturing-first sequence for turning operational friction into a system your team can actually use. Most engagements run in overlapping phases across roughly 10–16 weeks, depending on scope and readiness.

I
Investigate · 1–2 weeks

Investigate leakages

Find where time, material, money and decisions are slipping through the cracks—and isolate the few bottlenecks that matter most.

M
Map · 1–3 weeks

Map the real workflow

Trace how work moves today. Simplify handoffs and agree the operating rules before configuring tools around them.

P
Platform · 4–8 weeks

Put the data together

Connect the essential order, inventory, purchase, production and finance flows into a dependable system of record.

A
Activate · 1–2 weeks

Activate visibility

Turn transactions into useful signals: commitments at risk, shortages, exceptions and decisions that need attention.

C
Control · 2–4 weeks

Control routine work

Route approvals, alerts and recurring work to the right people while preserving the controls leadership needs.

T
Transfer · throughout

Transfer the rhythm

Train the team, review adoption and make the daily operating rhythm sustainable after go-live.

Manufacturing reference scenarios

Three operating problems. Three clearer ways to run them.

These are representative reference scenarios built from Cimulink’s manufacturing implementation playbooks. They show the kinds of operating questions an ERPNext solution can be designed to answer; outcomes depend on each business’s data, process and adoption.

Jewellery manufacturing

High-value material, visible at every stage.

Gold, stones and semi-finished goods move through stores, production and job work. The operating view links material issued, expected returns, wastage and the customer order behind the work.

Can you answer where your high-value material is—without calling five people?
Make-to-order industrial

Customer commitments connected to their dependencies.

For custom pumps, skids and assemblies, a promised date depends on BOMs, incoming material, work orders, testing and dispatch. The focus is on the exceptions that put a customer commitment at risk.

If a customer calls now, can your team explain the delivery date with confidence?
Repetitive & packaging

See line interruptions before the line stops.

Demand plans, resin availability, output, scrap, quality holds and supplier dates are brought into one operating view—so a line-level issue can be investigated before it disrupts the schedule.

Which material, line or quality exception is most likely to interrupt tomorrow’s plan?
